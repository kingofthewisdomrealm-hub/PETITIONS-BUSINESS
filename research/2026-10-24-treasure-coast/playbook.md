## The short version

You can build this business on the Treasure Coast, but not the way it worked in 2015. Florida has bolted most of the big doors shut. The money is in the side doors.

**What's mostly closed:**

- **Statewide amendments.** A federal court upheld HB 1205 in April 2026. Circulators must be registered Florida residents and citizens, no per-signature pay, 10-day turn-in, $50,000 fines per bad circulator. Only big-money sponsors can play.
- **Anything about zoning or a specific development.** Florida law bans initiatives and referendums on development orders, land development rules, and comp plan changes. A neighbor fight over a rezoning cannot go to the ballot.
- **Paid recalls of city officials.** Paying someone to circulate or witness a municipal recall petition is a crime under Florida law. Volunteers only.

**What's open (where you get paid):**

1. **Pressure petitions** (non-binding). A business or neighborhood group proves to a council or the Legislature that voters are mad. Your bread and butter.
2. **County charter initiatives in Brevard** (and St. Lucie, once you confirm its rules). Real ballot power on non-land-use topics. Expensive, so clients are serious.
3. **City charter initiatives** where the city charter allows them. Same land-use ban applies.
4. **Private statutory petitions.** Condo owners and mobile-home owners organizing.
5. **Candidate qualifying petitions for 2028.** Candidates skip the filing fee by collecting signatures from 1% of voters.
6. **Subcontracting to 2028 statewide sponsors** as a compliant Treasure Coast field team.

**The one-sentence pitch:** You don't sell petitions. You sell proof that voters are on the client's side, in a form politicians can't ignore.

## The Florida rules that decide what you can sell

Have a Florida election lawyer sign off on each campaign before money moves. Not legal advice.

**The 15-minute legal filter:** (1) Is it about zoning, a development, building rules or the comp plan? Yes → no ballot, sell a pressure petition. (2) Is it removing an elected city or county official? Yes → recall, volunteers only. (3) Does a city or county charter give voters initiative power on this? Yes → charter initiative, feasibility first. No → pressure petition to the council or Legislature.

**The land-use wall.** [Section 163.3167(8)](https://florida.public.law/statutes/fla._stat._163.3167) bans initiatives and referendums on any development order and any land development regulation, and on comp plan and map changes unless a city charter specifically allowed it by June 1, 2011. A citizen-led county charter amendment can't preempt development orders, land rules, comp plans or voluntary annexation unless the charter allowed it by January 1, 2024.

**The recall wall.** Nobody may pay someone to circulate or witness a municipal recall petition. Second-degree misdemeanor.

**Public money.** Cities and counties generally can't spend tax dollars campaigning for a ballot question. Your client is the private coalition, not city hall.

Quote math: valid needed = % × registered voters; raw = valid ÷ 0.70–0.80; field hours = raw ÷ signatures per hour; labor = hours × loaded rate; verification = raw × county fee; add legal, training, printing, data, 15% risk buffer, then your fee. Drafts only; nothing goes out until approved. Open with their problem; your 10 years go in line three. Ladder: free 20-minute call → 15-minute legal filter → sell Feasibility before the call ends → deliver in person with a Launch proposal → Launch contract with attorney engaged and deposit paid → upgrade to Full Service at the first win → case study and testimonial.
