The House: Treasure Coast

A casino doesn't win because it's lucky. It wins because it picks the games, sets the prices, gets paid on every hand, and never lets one gambler sink it. Run the petition business the same way and you can't lose over time, even when single campaigns do.

House math: the 8 rules that make the house win

House edge

Get paid on every hand

Charge for the Feasibility study up front, every time. Win or lose, the house got its cut. Price by time and milestones, never by whether the client wins.

The cage

Cash before chips

50% deposit to start. Verification fees ($3.60 to $4.24 per petition in Brevard) paid before you file. A casino never lends you chips and hopes.

Game mix

Slots pay the rent

Small, fast, repeatable jobs (candidate petitions, pressure sprints) keep the lights on. Tables pay the staff. Whales pay for growth. Run all three rooms.

Bankroll

Never bet the house on one whale

Keep any one client under about a third of your revenue. The biggest wallet on this floor is under criminal investigation. Whales leave. Rent doesn't.

Eye in the sky

Watch every signature

Check every sheet before turn-in. Florida's AG opened 46 probes into one sponsor's circulators. In a casino, cheating gets you banned. In petitions, it gets you indicted.

Closed tables

Don't deal illegal games

No referendums on rezonings or building rules. No paid recall circulators. No per-signature pay. The house doesn't run games the state can raid.

Comps

Give the free buffet

A free 20-minute plan ("1,000 clicks are worth zero; 1,000 voters are worth a vote") gets people through the door. The study is where they start paying.

Players' club

Bring them back every cycle

Every campaign ends with a case study and a testimonial. Elections come back every two years, and so do your best clients.

Closed tablesThe house doesn't deal these. Say no in the first 15 minutes and sell the legal version instead.
  • Rezoning or building-rule referendums (like tightening Cocoa Beach's 45-foot limit): banned by Fla. Stat. 163.3167(8).
  • Paid recall circulators: a crime under Fla. Stat. 100.361. Volunteers only.
  • Per-signature pay: a felony for statewide petitions.
  • Port St. Lucie's Riverland complex: rezoned Aug. 24. The table closed before you sat down.
  • Stuart's ballfield amendment: the commission put it on the ballot itself. No buyer.
